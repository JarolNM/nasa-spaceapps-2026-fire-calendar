Method

How does it work?

1

Two different eyes

MODIS (since 2000) sees 1 km pixels. VIIRS (since 2012) sees 375 m pixels and detects smaller fires.

2

Clean

We remove duplicates, clip to Tolima with DANE boundaries, and hide the volcano and static industrial sources.

3

Calibrate

From 2012 to 2025 both watch Tolima: VIIRS sees ~6 times more. We measure it by zone and month (k).

4

One single series

Before 2012, MODIS×k; since 2012, VIIRS. That way 26 years share the same scale.

Technical details and limitations

Data. NASA FIRMS standard products MODIS C6.1 (Terra and Aqua; Giglio et al. 2016) and VIIRS S-NPP 375 m (Schroeder et al. 2014). NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 are not included: they start in 2018 and 2024 and would create artificial jumps in the series. That is why the FIRMS viewer may show more points for the same fire. Confidence: VIIRS low/nominal/high; MODIS <30 low, 30–79 nominal, ≥80 high.

Harmonization. k = Σ VIIRS / Σ MODIS over 2012–2025 per latitude band (north ≥4.5°, center, south <3.75°) and month, with the active filters. Anomalies are computed against each period's climatology, separately for the MODIS era (2001–2011) and the VIIRS era (2012–2025).

Limitations. A hotspot is a thermal detection at overpass time, not burned area. Cloud cover and overpass time matter. k is assumed constant over time. The year 2000 is almost empty.

Expert review by a geologist

On 15 Nov 2026 our team geologist labeled 53 random hotspots with the etiquetar.html tool, checking Sentinel-2/Landsat images before and after, burn scars, land cover and local news.

Our classificationReal fireFalseUnsure
Volcano / static source (hidden by default)121 ✓0
Vegetation fire18 ✓94

21 of 22 points we hid as volcano or industry were confirmed false (Nevado del Ruiz, Caracolito cement kiln, rice-husk burning at Espinal mills). Of the vegetation points with a clear verdict, 67 % (18/27) were real fires, mostly in croplands and pastures during the dry season and El Niño. The 9 false ones (bare soil, no burn scar or news) were removed from the fire events (data/falsos_positivos.json); the labeled sample is in data/etiquetas_geologa_2026-11-15.csv.

Transparency

Use of AI (SI) tools in this project

We used an AI assistant (Anthropic Claude) under the direction of the GeoElectronic team. Everything AI-assisted is marked with ✦ AI labels on the page and in file metadata.

  • Images and animation: the Sati mascot, the intro scene and the 7-step explainer animation are SVG drawn with AI assistance; each carries a visible “AI-generated” watermark. Satellite images are not AI-generated: they are real NASA/USGS Landsat, Copernicus Sentinel-2, NASA HLS and Esri observations.
  • Audio: Sati’s voice is AI-generated speech (Microsoft neural text-to-speech, voice en-US-JennyNeural, via the open-source edge-tts library). Each MP3 carries this statement in its ID3 metadata (title/comment), and audio/sati/ABOUT.md describes it.
  • Code: the web app (index.html) and the Python pipeline (pipeline/*.py) were written with AI assistance and reviewed and tested by the team; each file states it in a header comment and in a meta name="ai-assistance" tag.
  • Data: the hotspots, imagery and climate values are real, unmodified observations (NASA FIRMS, Landsat, Sentinel-2, NOAA, DANE). The derived products (harmonized series, fire events, quarterly composites, industry catalog) were computed by AI-assisted scripts; their JSON metadata include an ai_assisted flag. Industry descriptions were researched with AI assistance and cite their sources.
  • Text: interface texts, Sati’s messages and the explainer narration were drafted with AI assistance and checked against the cited references.
  • No NASA logos, insignia, flags or mission identifiers were generated or modified.
